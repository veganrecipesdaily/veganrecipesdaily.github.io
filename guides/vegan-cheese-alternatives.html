Vegan Cheese Alternatives

Cheese is the food many people miss most when they go vegan, but there are more alternatives now than ever before. Some are made from nuts, some from soy or coconut, and some are simply flavourings that give a cheesy taste. This guide explains the options, which ones work for which dish, and how to make your own.

Nutritional yeast: the vegan cheese flavour

Nutritional yeast is the single most useful vegan cheese alternative. It is a deactivated yeast with a savoury, cheesy flavour, and it is rich in B vitamins, including B12 if it is fortified. Sprinkle it on pasta, popcorn, and roasted vegetables, stir it into sauces, or blend it into cashew cheese. Two tablespoons provide about 8 grams of protein. It is inexpensive, lasts for months in the cupboard, and is available in most supermarkets and health food shops. If you buy one vegan pantry item, make it this one.

Cashew and nut cheeses

Cashew cheese is the gold standard of homemade vegan cheese. Soak raw cashews for a few hours, blend them with water, lemon juice, garlic, and nutritional yeast, and you get a smooth, creamy cheese that works as a spread, a pasta sauce, or a pizza topping. Almonds make a firmer cheese that can be sliced. For a baked cheese, add tapioca starch to the cashew base, which makes it stretchy when heated. Nut cheeses take a little time, but they taste far better than most shop-bought options and contain no additives.

Shop-bought vegan cheese

Shop-bought vegan cheese has improved a lot, but quality varies. Some melt well on pizza and toast, while others stay rubbery or taste of coconut. Look for cheeses made from nuts or soy, and check the ingredients for excessive additives. Taste a few brands to find one you like, and use them where melting matters most — on pizza, in toasties, and on burgers. For sandwiches and salads, a good hummus or avocado often works better than a slice of vegan cheese. Keep expectations realistic: vegan cheese is a useful ingredient, not an exact copy of dairy cheese.

Cheesy dishes without cheese

Many dishes can be cheesy without any cheese at all. A pasta sauce made from blended cashews and nutritional yeast tastes convincingly cheesy. A cauliflower cheese sauce, made from blended cauliflower, nutritional yeast, and mustard, is creamy and comforting. On pizza, use a rich tomato sauce, plenty of vegetables, and a drizzle of cashew cream instead of cheese. In a toastie, use hummus, avocado, and roasted vegetables. Once you learn these tricks, you will find you miss cheese far less than you expected.

A vegan cheese board

A vegan cheese board is a good way to introduce friends to plant-based cheese. Include two or three different styles: a soft cashew cheese, a firm nut cheese, and a shop-bought melting cheese. Add crackers, bread, grapes, apple slices, nuts, and olives, and let people taste and compare. Serve the cheeses at room temperature, since cold mutes their flavour. Add a chutney or a jam for sweetness, and a glass of wine or sparkling water. A cheese board is social food, and it works because people try small amounts of many things, which is the best way to discover what they like.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is nutritional yeast?

It is a deactivated yeast with a savoury, cheesy flavour. It is rich in B vitamins and protein, and it is used to add a cheesy taste to sauces, pasta, and popcorn.

Does vegan cheese melt?

Some do, some do not. Cheeses made with coconut oil and starch usually melt, while nut-based cheeses soften rather than melt. Check the packet and try different brands.

Can I make vegan cheese at home?

Yes, easily. Blend soaked cashews with lemon, garlic, and nutritional yeast for a creamy cheese, or add tapioca starch for a stretchy, meltable version.
