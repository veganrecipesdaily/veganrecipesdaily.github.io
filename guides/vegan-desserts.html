Vegan Desserts

Vegan desserts can be rich, creamy, and impressive, and nobody at the table needs to know they are plant-based. The secret is using ingredients that naturally create the textures people love: coconut cream for richness, cashews for creaminess, and ripe fruit for sweetness. This guide covers the key techniques and a set of reliable recipes.

Creamy desserts without dairy

Coconut cream is the easiest route to rich vegan desserts. Chill a tin of full-fat coconut milk overnight, scoop out the thick cream on top, and whip it with vanilla and a little sugar for a topping that behaves like whipped cream. Soaked cashews blended with water, lemon, and sweetener make a smooth base for cheesecakes and mousses. Silken tofu blends into chocolate mousse with cocoa and maple syrup, and the result is silky and light. Aquafaba, the liquid from tinned chickpeas, whips into stiff peaks for meringues and mousses, and it is the key to a proper vegan pavlova.

Cakes, brownies, and cookies

Vegan chocolate cake is one of the easiest cakes to make, because cocoa and oil do most of the work and no eggs are needed for a moist crumb. Brownies made with dark chocolate, oil, and flax eggs are fudgy and rich. For cookies, use vegan butter or coconut oil, chill the dough, and bake until the edges are set but the centres are still soft. Fruit crumbles are naturally easy to make vegan: top stewed fruit with oats, flour, sugar, and coconut oil. Always check that chocolate, biscuits, and sprinkles are dairy-free, since many contain milk.

Ice cream and frozen treats

Homemade vegan ice cream is simple with a blender and a freezer. Frozen bananas blended on their own make a soft-serve texture that is surprisingly like ice cream. For a richer result, blend coconut cream with cocoa, vanilla, and maple syrup, then freeze, stirring every hour to keep it smooth. Fruit sorbet needs only frozen fruit, a little sugar, and lemon juice. Ice lollies made from blended fruit and coconut milk are a quick summer treat, and they are easy to make with children.

Serving and storing

Vegan desserts often taste best at room temperature, so take cakes and cheesecakes out of the fridge thirty minutes before serving. Store cakes in an airtight container for three to four days, and freeze slices individually for longer storage. Cheesecakes keep well in the freezer for a month and defrost in the fridge overnight. Label anything you freeze with the name and date. When serving guests, present the dessert confidently without announcing that it is vegan — let the taste speak for itself.

Desserts for special occasions

For a special occasion, a vegan dessert can be the centrepiece of the meal. A chocolate cake with ganache, a baked cheesecake with a berry topping, or a pavlova with coconut cream and fresh fruit all look impressive and taste wonderful. Make the dessert a day ahead where you can, so the day itself is relaxed. Serve it confidently, without announcing that it is vegan, and let your guests discover it for themselves. If you are catering for mixed guests, make one dessert that everyone can eat, rather than a separate vegan dessert that singles anyone out. A good dessert brings people together, and nobody needs to know how it was made until they ask for the recipe.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can vegan desserts taste as good as regular ones?

Yes. Chocolate cake, brownies, and cheesecake made with cashews or coconut cream are often indistinguishable from dairy versions, especially when served at room temperature.

What replaces eggs in vegan desserts?

Flax eggs work for cakes and cookies, aquafaba whips into meringue, and silken tofu blends into mousse. Choose the replacement that fits the recipe.

How do I make vegan whipped cream?

Chill a tin of full-fat coconut milk overnight, scoop out the solid cream, and whip it with vanilla and a little sugar. It holds its shape well for a few hours.
