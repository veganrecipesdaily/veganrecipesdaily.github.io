Vegan Snacks

Snacks are where a vegan diet can go wrong, because the easiest options are often crisps, biscuits, and sweets. With a little planning, vegan snacks can be healthy, filling, and genuinely enjoyable. This guide lists the best options for different situations, from desk drawers to lunchboxes to evenings on the sofa.

Healthy everyday snacks

Fruit is the simplest vegan snack: an apple, a banana, or a handful of grapes needs no preparation. Add a handful of nuts for protein and fat, and the snack becomes far more filling. Rice cakes with peanut butter, hummus with vegetable sticks, and a small pot of edamame are all quick and nutritious. Roasted chickpeas, seasoned with paprika and baked until crisp, are a crunchy, high-protein snack that keeps for a week in a jar. Keep a few of these in your bag or desk drawer so you are never caught hungry.

Sweet snacks

For a sweet snack, try dates stuffed with peanut butter, a small handful of dark chocolate, or a banana with a drizzle of nut butter. Energy balls, made from dates, oats, cocoa, and nuts, take ten minutes to make and keep in the fridge for a week. Fruit with coconut yoghurt is light and refreshing. Vegan biscuits and cakes are fine as an occasional treat, but check the label — many are high in sugar and low in nutrients. A smoothie can also work as a snack if it is small and balanced, with protein and healthy fats.

Snacks for children

Children need snacks that are fun, filling, and easy to eat. Fruit kebabs, made from chunks of melon, berries, and grapes on a stick, are popular. Mini sandwiches with peanut butter and banana, or hummus and cucumber, work well in lunchboxes. Popcorn, lightly salted, is a wholegrain snack that children love. Homemade muffins, made with banana and oats, are a good lunchbox addition. Avoid giving children too many dried fruits, which are sticky and can damage teeth, and always include a protein or fat with fruit to keep them full.

Evening and party snacks

In the evening, when the habit of snacking is strongest, choose snacks that feel like a treat. Guacamole with tortilla chips, hummus with pitta bread, and roasted nuts with spices are all satisfying. A bowl of popcorn with nutritional yeast tastes cheesy and is low in calories. For parties, make a big batch of bruschetta with tomato and basil, or a platter of vegetables with dips. If you are watching your weight, pre-portion snacks into small bowls rather than eating from the packet, and eat slowly so you notice when you are full.

Snacks to avoid

Not every vegan snack is a good snack. Crisps, biscuits, and sweets are vegan, but they are high in salt, sugar, or fat, and low in nutrients. Vegan snack bars are often expensive and full of sweeteners. Dried fruit is nutritious, but it is sticky and high in sugar, so eat it in small amounts. Flavoured popcorn can be high in salt and sugar. The best rule is to snack on real food — fruit, nuts, vegetables, and hummus — and to treat packaged snacks as an occasional pleasure rather than a daily habit. Read the label once, and if sugar, salt, or fat is very high, save that snack for a special occasion.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the healthiest vegan snack?

Fruit with nuts, hummus with vegetables, or roasted chickpeas. All three give fibre, protein, and healthy fats, and they keep you full.

Are vegan crisps and biscuits healthy?

Not usually. Many are high in salt, sugar, or fat, even if they are vegan. They are fine as an occasional treat, but not as an everyday snack.

How do I stop snacking too much in the evening?

Eat a proper dinner with enough protein and fat, keep snacks out of sight, and pre-portion what you eat. If you are genuinely hungry, have a small, balanced snack.
