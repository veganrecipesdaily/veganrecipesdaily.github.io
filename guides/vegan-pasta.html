Vegan Pasta

Pasta is one of the easiest vegan meals, and it is naturally plant-based in most cases. With a good sauce, plenty of vegetables, and a protein source, pasta becomes a complete and satisfying dinner. This guide covers the classic vegan pasta dishes, creamy sauces without dairy, and the tips that make pasta night a success.

Classic tomato pasta

A simple tomato pasta is the foundation of vegan pasta cooking. Cook garlic and chilli in olive oil, add tinned tomatoes, simmer for twenty minutes, and season with salt and basil. Add white beans or lentils for protein, or roasted vegetables for extra flavour. A puttanesca sauce, with olives, capers, and tomatoes, is salty and bold and needs no cheese. Arrabbiata, with chilli and garlic, is another quick classic. These sauces are ready in the time it takes to cook the pasta, which makes them perfect weeknight dinners.

Creamy pasta without dairy

Creamy vegan pasta sauces are easy once you know the tricks. Soaked cashews blended with water, garlic, and nutritional yeast make a rich, cheesy sauce that coats pasta beautifully. Coconut milk with garlic and mushrooms makes a creamy sauce for tagliatelle. A silken tofu sauce, blended with lemon and herbs, is light and protein-rich. For a quick creamy pasta, toss hot pasta with olive oil, garlic, and a spoonful of nutritional yeast, which gives a cheesy flavour. These sauces are often lighter than dairy cream sauces and just as satisfying.

Pesto and vegetable pasta

Pesto is naturally almost vegan — just replace the parmesan with nutritional yeast. Blend basil, pine nuts or walnuts, garlic, olive oil, and nutritional yeast for a classic pesto. A roasted red pepper pesto, or a sun-dried tomato pesto, are good variations. For vegetable pasta, roast courgette, peppers, and aubergine, then toss them with pasta, olive oil, and herbs. A pasta primavera, with spring vegetables and lemon, is light and fresh. Add toasted nuts or seeds on top for crunch and extra protein.

Pasta tips and pairings

Use plenty of salted water to cook pasta, and save a cup of the cooking water — its starch helps sauces cling to the pasta. Cook pasta until just al dente, since it keeps cooking in the sauce. Serve pasta with a side salad and bread, and keep portions moderate, around 80 to 100 grams of dry pasta per person. Wholewheat pasta adds fibre and is a good everyday choice. Check that fresh pasta is egg-free if you buy it ready-made, and remember that dried pasta is usually vegan.

Pasta for a crowd

Pasta is the easiest food to cook for a crowd, but it needs a little care. Use a very large pot with plenty of salted water, and do not add oil to the water — it stops the sauce sticking to the pasta. Cook the pasta in batches if your pot is small, and keep it warm in the sauce rather than in the water. Make the sauce in a large pan, and toss the pasta through it just before serving. Serve salad and bread on the side, and let people help themselves. A big bowl of pasta, a salad, and a bottle of olive oil is a generous meal for any number of guests, and it costs very little per person.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is pasta vegan?

Most dried pasta is vegan, made from just flour and water. Fresh pasta often contains egg, so check the label.

How do I make vegan pasta creamy?

Blend soaked cashews with water and garlic, use coconut milk, or toss hot pasta with olive oil and nutritional yeast. All three work well.

What protein should I add to pasta?

White beans, lentils, chickpeas, tofu, or tempeh all work. Nutritional yeast also adds protein and a cheesy flavour.
