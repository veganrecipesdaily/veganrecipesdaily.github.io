Vegan Soups

Soup is one of the simplest and most forgiving things to cook, and vegan soups are often the best of all. Lentils, beans, and vegetables make them hearty and nutritious, and a pot of soup can feed a family for days. This guide covers the techniques and the classic recipes that every vegan cook should know.

The basics of soup making

Every good soup starts with the same foundation: onion, garlic, and celery or carrot, cooked gently in olive oil until soft. Add your main ingredients, cover with stock or water, simmer until tender, and season well. A bay leaf, a sprig of thyme, or a pinch of cumin adds depth. Taste and adjust the salt at the end, and finish with a drizzle of olive oil, a squeeze of lemon, or a handful of fresh herbs. A hand blender turns a chunky soup into a smooth one in seconds, and a little coconut milk or blended cashews can make a soup creamy without dairy.

Classic vegan soups

Lentil soup is the classic: red lentils, carrots, onion, and cumin, simmered for thirty minutes and blended until smooth. Tomato soup with white beans is rich and filling, especially with a swirl of coconut cream. Minestrone, the Italian vegetable and bean soup, is endlessly variable and uses whatever vegetables are in season. Butternut squash soup with ginger is a warming autumn favourite. A spicy black bean soup with lime and coriander is a quick weeknight dinner. Each of these is easy to double and freeze.

Creamy soups without dairy

Creamy soups do not need cream. Blend cooked cauliflower, potato, or white beans into the soup for a silky texture. Coconut milk adds richness to curried soups. Soaked cashews blended with water make a neutral cream that works in any soup. A spoonful of nutritional yeast adds a savoury, cheesy depth. For a chunky soup, stir in a spoonful of peanut butter or tahini at the end for richness. The trick is to blend only part of the soup, so you keep some texture while the rest becomes smooth and creamy.

Serving, storing, and freezing

Serve soup with crusty bread, a side salad, or a sprinkle of seeds and herbs. Most soups keep in the fridge for four to five days and freeze well for three months — freeze them in single portions in containers or bags, and defrost overnight in the fridge. Label each container with the soup name and date. Reheat gently on the stove, adding a splash of water if the soup has thickened. Soups often taste better the next day, as the flavours have time to develop, so making soup is one of the best uses of a Sunday afternoon.

Bread to serve with soup

Soup and bread are a natural pair, and the right bread makes the meal complete. A crusty sourdough is the classic choice, and it is usually vegan. Wholegrain bread adds fibre and is more filling. Flatbread, warmed in the oven, is good with curried soups. Garlic bread, made with olive oil and garlic instead of butter, is a favourite with tomato soup. For a lighter option, serve soup with crackers or breadsticks. If you bake your own bread, a simple no-knead loaf takes minutes of active work and tastes far better than shop-bought. Keep bread in a cloth bag so it stays fresh, and use stale bread for croutons or breadcrumbs rather than throwing it away.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I make soup filling enough for dinner?

Add lentils, beans, or potatoes, and serve with bread. A soup with a protein source and a starchy base is a complete meal.

Can I freeze vegan soup?

Yes. Most vegan soups freeze well for up to three months. Leave out any pasta or rice, which goes mushy, and add it fresh when reheating.

What stock should I use for vegan soup?

A good vegetable stock is best. You can use cubes, powder, or homemade stock made from vegetable peelings. Taste it first, since some stocks are very salty.
