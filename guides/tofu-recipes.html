Tofu Recipes

Tofu gets a bad reputation from people who have only eaten it badly cooked. Done well, it is crisp outside, tender inside, and full of flavour from marinades and sauces. This guide explains how to choose, press, and cook tofu, then shares ten recipes that show how versatile it really is.

Choosing and pressing tofu

Buy firm or extra-firm tofu for cooking; silken tofu is for smoothies, sauces, and desserts, not frying. Pressing removes water so the tofu can absorb flavour and crisp up. Wrap the block in a clean tea towel, put a plate on top, and weigh it down with a tin or a book for twenty to thirty minutes. If you cook tofu often, a tofu press is a worthwhile buy. After pressing, cut the tofu into cubes or slices, and always season or marinate it — plain tofu is bland by design, and that is its strength, because it takes on any flavour you give it.

Cooking methods that work

Pan-frying is the most popular method: toss cubes in a little cornflour, fry in hot oil until golden on all sides, then add sauce. Baking is easier for big batches — spread cubes on a tray with oil and soy sauce and bake at 200 degrees Celsius for twenty-five minutes, turning once. Air-frying gives a very crisp result with less oil. For stir-fries, fry the tofu first, set it aside, cook the vegetables, then add the tofu back with the sauce at the end so it stays crisp. Whichever method you choose, do not crowd the pan, or the tofu will steam instead of browning.

Marinades and sauces

A good marinade transforms tofu. A simple one is soy sauce, garlic, ginger, maple syrup, and sesame oil, left for at least fifteen minutes or overnight in the fridge. For a smoky flavour, add smoked paprika and a little liquid smoke. Peanut sauce, made from peanut butter, soy sauce, lime juice, and chilli, is a classic pairing. Barbecue sauce, curry paste, and teriyaki sauce all work well too. Marinate after pressing, and save some marinade to brush on while cooking for extra flavour.

Ten tofu recipes to try

Start with a simple tofu stir-fry with vegetables and noodles, then try crispy baked tofu in a Buddha bowl with tahini dressing. Tofu scramble with turmeric makes a great breakfast, and tofu curry with coconut milk is a comforting dinner. Mapo tofu, a spicy Sichuan dish, shows how well tofu takes bold flavours. Tofu katsu, coated in breadcrumbs and fried, is a crowd-pleaser, and tofu tacos with slaw are quick and fun. For something lighter, try tofu and vegetable skewers on the barbecue, or a tofu salad with peanut dressing. Silken tofu blends into chocolate mousse for dessert.

Storing and using leftover tofu

Leftover tofu needs proper storage. Once opened, keep tofu in fresh water in a sealed container in the fridge, and change the water daily. It will keep for three to four days. Cooked tofu keeps for four days in the fridge, and it is good cold in salads or reheated in stir-fries. If you have more tofu than you can use, freeze it: freezing changes the texture, making it chewier and more absorbent, which many people prefer. Defrost frozen tofu in the fridge overnight, then press it before cooking. Silken tofu, once opened, should be used within a day or two, and it is best in smoothies, sauces, and desserts rather than fried dishes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to press tofu?

Pressing is worth it for firm tofu that you plan to fry or bake, because it improves texture and flavour. For soups and scrambles, you can skip it.

How long does tofu last once opened?

Keep opened tofu in fresh water in the fridge, changing the water daily, and use it within three to four days. Unopened tofu keeps until its printed date.

Can I freeze tofu?

Yes, and freezing actually improves the texture, making it chewier and more absorbent. Defrost it in the fridge, then press it before cooking.
